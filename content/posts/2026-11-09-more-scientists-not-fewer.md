<p>A society that no longer needs your labor is the first in history that can afford to care whether you flourish. Duty societies needed your body. Rights societies needed your work. The machine that threatens to make us unnecessary can also provide the surplus that makes nurture possible at scale.</p><p>Whether we use it that way is a choice, and the first step is realizing there is one. Automation can restore the original arrow: society as a machine that serves people, instead of people serving as the machine. None of this is guaranteed, and make no mistake about it, a duty society with robots is a dystopia.</p><p>So what would humans do? We would be crafters, learners, entertainers, engineers, and explorers. Not because we'll do those things better than machines, but because human limits are unknown. A computer beat the world chess champion in 1997, and chess is more popular now than ever.</p><p>And output stops being the purpose. Someone studies physics because the universe is astonishing; if she develops the polymer that makes an orbital skyhook possible, wonderful — but that's extra.</p><p>Here is the counterintuitive outcome: a nurture society would have more scientists and more thinkers, not fewer. It's well documented how many brilliant minds drop out of STEM majors because of knockout courses and strict grading curves. Does a person with encyclopedic memory make the doctor with the best bedside manner? Certainly not. Even in research, a creative C student could easily outdo the best memorizer at inventing new technology. On a macro scale, technology tends to accelerate because more people get to contribute to the brain trust.</p><p>In short, telling people they can study physics if they want is going to create more physicists than telling people that only the top 20% of physics students can get a job.</p><p>One caveat: this isn't a <em>Man of Steel</em> Krypton, where the Codex assigns every person's role before birth. That's just the duty-based society with an extra helping of paternalism. The goal is self-actualization: society helping each person reach their fullest potential.</p><p>So are humans just holding on? Only if we keep asking to be needed. The future isn't empty. It's unnamed. And for the first time, it can afford us.</p><p><strong>This is the last of five Episode 3 quick reads.
