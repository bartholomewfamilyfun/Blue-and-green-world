<p>Every week brings another headline: AI is coming for your job, automation is coming for your industry. The labor market is shifting, and quickly. But the fear that the machines are coming for us isn't new. It's wearing a new outfit.</p><p>The last time an entire class of people had zero leverage, zero capital, and every institutional door closed, the outcome 150 years later wasn't collapse. It was partnership in some of the largest industries on the planet. That isn't a feel-good story. It's a mechanism — and a mechanism can be studied, repeated, and improved.</p><p>To understand what an unrecognizable technology will do in fifty years, don't stare at the technology. Find a moment when the outcome was unimaginable to the people living through it, and reverse-engineer how it happened. In eight steps:</p><ol><li><p><strong>Powerless at the precipice.</strong> In 1865, four million people were freed, and their labor finally belonged to them.</p></li><li><p><strong>Dismal prospects.</strong> No land, no capital, restricted education, gatekept professions.</p></li><li><p><strong>Opportunity presents.</strong> Talent flows where gatekeeping fails. You can deny a loan quietly; you can't deny who crossed the finish line first. Thirteen of the fifteen jockeys in the first Kentucky Derby, in 1875, were Black.</p></li><li><p><strong>The mechanism is perverted.</strong> Black jockeys were pushed out, Jack Johnson's 1908 title triggered a national panic, and the Mann Act followed.</p></li><li><p><strong>Concentration of resources.</strong> The Great Migration built density in northern cities, and basketball, which needs almost no land or equipment, landed in the communities with the least capital.</p></li><li><p><strong>Heroes arise.</strong> Segregation forced parallel institutions, and outsiders like Eddie Gottlieb and Abe Saperstein helped fund them.</p></li><li><p><strong>Mechanisms feed mechanisms.</strong> MTV barely played Black artists until Walter Yetnikoff threatened to pull his label's roster unless it aired Michael Jackson.</p></li><li><p><strong>The impossible future.</strong> From spirituals in a cotton field to Marian Anderson singing at the Lincoln Memorial.</p></li></ol><p><strong>Step four is where the mechanism turns on the people it was lifting, and step six is who kept it alive. Read <em><a href="/posts/the-square-block/">The Square Block</a></em> and <em><a href="/posts/who-kept-the-lights-on/">Who Kept the Lights On</a></em> in the 1865 Exercise.</strong></p>
